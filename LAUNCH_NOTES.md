## 2. Draft for Reddit (e.g., r/MachineLearning, r/learnmachinelearning)

> **Title:** I created a visual, intuition-first guide from Python to modern AI research (micrograd, nanoGPT, DPO, and GRPO loss)
>
> **Body:**
> Most machine learning roadmaps either overwhelm beginners with dry mathematical notation without explaining why things work, or rely entirely on high-level black-box libraries (`model.fit()`).
>
> I wanted to create a structured path inspired by the hands-on spirit of Asabeneh's 30 Days of Python:
> - 20 numbered topics covering Tooling, Linear Algebra, Calculus, Classical ML, Micrograd from scratch, PyTorch, Tokenization, Attention, building a decoder-only GPT, Reinforcement Learning, and modern post-training.
> - Plain-English intuition and visual diagrams before any formal equations.
> - Every jargon term defined immediately in parentheses.
> - Runnable Python/PyTorch scripts for every module, plus graded revision exercises (L1 concept recall, L2 implementation with tests, L3 research deep dive).
> - Walks through the exact mathematical loss mechanics behind DeepSeekMath's Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
>
> It's completely free and open-source under MIT (excluding the anime banner image, which has a clear copyright disclaimer in `ASSETS_NOTICE.md`). Would love feedback from learners and researchers: https://github.com/amirlankalm/ai-research-foundations
